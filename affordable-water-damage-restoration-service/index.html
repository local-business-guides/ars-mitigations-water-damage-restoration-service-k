How the work and schedule are determined

A restoration project generally begins with understanding where water has reached and what materials may be affected. Depending on the findings, the work can involve removing water, drying affected areas, cleaning, and arranging repairs. Mold remediation may be relevant in some situations, but the need for it depends on the conditions found. Ask the restoration provider to describe the steps for your property.

There is no universal schedule for this work. The amount of water, the affected materials, and the extent of repairs all influence timing. A few common terms can help: mitigation refers to steps intended to limit additional damage; remediation is work to address an issue such as mold; and restoration is the broader effort to make affected areas usable again. If a term appears in a proposed scope, ask what work it represents.
